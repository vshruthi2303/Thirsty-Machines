Thirsty Machines

Thirsty Machines is a living archive of stories relating to data centers around the world. They are grouped by recurring themes such as lack of transparency, corruption and misinformation. This project aims to detangle the mystery of the environmental impact of GenAI.

mason county · Corruption

No Name Given (Mason County)

Unnamed buyers offered a Kentucky farming family $10M for their land, refusing to say who they were unless an NDA was signed first. The family said no, but eighteen neighbors said yes. The public didn’t learn a data center project existed there until months later, at an unrelated meeting.

What else has already been decided before anyone gets a vote?

chile jobs · Misinformation

Mostly Security And Cleaning (Chile jobs)

Microsoft and Google promised thousands of jobs; but in reality they only offered a dozen positions and mostly in security and cleaning rather than skilled IT roles. The infrastructure itself just doesn’t need many people.

Who was the jobs promise actually made for?

energy per query · Misinformation

No One Will Say (Energy per query)

Asked directly, Google, OpenAI, and Microsoft all declined to share real energy figures for a single AI query. Researchers estimate instead, using open-source proxies as substitutes.

If the industry itself won’t reveal one number, how can we ever know what any of this actually costs?

generator permits · Misinformation + Transparency

Found By A Permit (Generator permits)

Every data center needs a backup generator permit — a record impossible to hide. Filing public records requests revealed data centres across the country. It also found that often a shell company name was used.

If a permit for a generator can unmask a company, what else is hiding behind paperwork nobody thinks to check?

Ordered To Release (Roanoke)

A judge ordered a Virginia water authority to release records tied to a possible Google data center. The authority may appeal rather than comply.

Why should it take a judge to find out how much water is leaving your town?

ypsilanti · Corruption

Next Door To The Bomb (Ypsilanti)

A university’s data center partnership with a nuclear weapons lab was announced as an “AI research facility.” Because it’s a university project, standard zoning review never applied.

When the normal process for public input doesn’t exist, how is anyone supposed to push back?

zeewolde · Misinformation + Greenwashing

Twenty Jobs, Not Four Hundred (Zeewolde)

Meta’s promised 410 jobs and “free heat” sustainability claim both fell apart under scrutiny. Local pressure triggered a nationwide moratorium — and the company relocated elsewhere.

If resistance in one place just moves the problem, what would it take to win the fight once and for all?

chile / google · Transparency

Water For 80,000, On Paper (Chile / Google)

The raw water use figure was findable because Chilean environmental review law requires it to be filed. The meaning of the figure. 7.6 million liters/day means very little to a resident until translated into “the annual water use of 80,000+ people” — a comparison the company’s own filing did not make.

What does it mean for information to be available but practically out of reach for the people it concerns?

amazon water · Transparency + Greenwashing

Three Years Of Silence (Amazon water)

A leaked 2022 memo shows Amazon weighing which headlines to avoid before disclosing its water use. A number finally came in 2025 — 2.5 billion gallons — wrapped in a comparison against a narrower competitor figure. Reports also described water replenishment projects as offsetting consumption instead of restoration efforts.

If the silence lasted three years, how would anyone know when the next one starts?

indiana / anthropic · Transparency

Farmland, Then Chips (Indiana / Anthropic)

Amazon’s largest AI data center — 30 buildings, half a million chips. It sits on land that was farmland a year earlier, running the systems behind Anthropic’s models. Its real water use isn’t confirmed.

Thirty buildings went up in a year. Why is its water use still not a public number?

querétaro · Corruption

A Drought The Court Couldn't Stop (Querétaro)

14.8% of the local population lacks consistent potable water access, while 10 billion dollars is being invested in data centres in the region over the next decade. Even formal legal escalation did not slow development.

When legal escalation stops working, what’s left for a community to do?

ekurhuleni · Transparency

Nobody Went Looking (Ekurhuleni)

A data center opened near Johannesburg in a water- and power-strained region. No leak, no lawsuit, no investigation — most residents never learned it was there.

The water is gone either way. Who was supposed to be watching?
